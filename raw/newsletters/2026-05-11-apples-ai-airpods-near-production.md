## **AI needs power — but a growing number of communities want to pull the plug**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/b48183e3-6e62-478f-9e46-450ebc2373e1/superhumanteam_A_robot_pulling_the_plug_on_a_large_factory_--_e736d7c2-b0a9-4b4e-afcd-c1dccc7828fa_0.jpg?t=1778452053)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**Data center boom.** Brookfield [estimates](https://www.brookfield.com/ai-opportunities) that $2 trillion will flow into data center development by 2030 as Big Tech sprints to meet AI demand. While these sprawling facilities help power AI innovation, they’re increasingly being tied to [rising](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/05/many-americans-hold-utility-companies-responsible-for-their-rising-home-energy-bills/?cb_viewport=mobile) energy prices and water overuse — and community pushback is reaching a crescendo.

**The backlash.** Local opposition has blocked or delayed 20 projects over the past three months, impacting $98 billion in potential investment. That’s more than all disruptions tracked since 2023. The pushback is becoming a bipartisan issue, with both parties voicing support for increased oversight.

**Proposed solutions.** 27 states are advancing legislation. The most common goal? To make developers cover energy costs and disclose usage. Others are floating bigger swings, like a [compute tax](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/job-losses-ai-compute-tax-ubi-89b7802e?st=R1sJjT&mod=superhuman) on providers that would fund social services to offset the impacts of AI.

**Growing tensions.** With midterms six months out, expect this issue to continue climbing higher on the political agenda until a workable solution is found. Want to track data center projects in your area? Explore the [Data Center Proposal Tracker](https://trackdatacenters.com/).

## **How to create educational encyclopedia style images**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/edbc45d4-8762-444d-8ad1-3a77a3135abc/Figma_Group_1000005230.jpg?t=1778438706)

* Open [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) and sign in

* Select + > ‘Create Image’ > enter your prompt

```
Sample Prompt: Create a premium vertical “encyclopedia-style” infographic about [TOPIC] that looks like a collectible natural-history handbook page mixed with modern editorial infographic design. The layout should feel highly structured, educational, and modular — not like a poster or advertisement. Include one large ultra-detailed main subject image, multiple zoomed-in detail callouts, rounded information panels, taxonomy/basic profile sections, behavior/ecology facts, physical characteristics, quick facts, comparison modules, rating systems, icons, charts, and concise encyclopedia-style text. Use a clean light background, elegant soft color palette, gentle shadows, refined typography, small scientific icons, and organized editorial spacing with high information density but comfortable readability. The design should resemble a publishable reference guide or museum-quality knowledge card designed for collecting and sharing on social media.
```
* Replace [TOPIC] with anything you want to learn about, for example: Black Holes, Ancient Egypt, Sharks, or Human Anatomy.

##### **PROMPT STATION**

## **Visual Thinking**

```
ChatGPT Prompt: Explain [TOPIC] using a highly structured visual-thinking approach that makes complex ideas easy to understand, remember, and mentally visualize. Break the topic into clear sections using text-based diagrams, flowcharts, layered frameworks, timelines, systems maps, comparisons, mental models, and step-by-step structures wherever relevant. Instead of giving only paragraphs of explanation, organize the information like a visual blueprint in written form showing how concepts connect, influence each other, and build progressively from simple foundations to advanced understanding. Use arrows, hierarchies, categories, and cause-and-effect relationships to simulate visual learning inside text. Prioritize clarity, intuitive understanding, and pattern recognition over jargon-heavy explanations. For abstract concepts, create analogies or real-world visual metaphors that make the idea easier to “see” mentally. If processes are involved, explain them like a flowchart with sequential stages and feedback loops. If systems are involved, explain inputs, outputs, dependencies, and interactions between components. Use concise labels, structured formatting, and visual spacing so the explanation feels like studying an interactive whiteboard rather than reading an essay. End with a simplified “mental snapshot” summary that compresses the entire topic into one easy-to-remember framework or visual scene I can instantly recall later.
```
