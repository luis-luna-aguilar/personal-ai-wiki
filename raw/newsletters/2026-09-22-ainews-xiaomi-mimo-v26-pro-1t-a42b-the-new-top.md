This is a first for the “Apple of China” phone maker-turned-frontier lab: “The MiMo-V2.6 series includes two natively omnimodal models: MiMo-V2.6-Pro is our most capable model to date, while MiMo-V2.6-Flash strikes the best balance between intelligence, efficiency, and cost. We are also rolling-out MiMo-V2.6-Pro-UltraSpeed, delivering up to 20x faster output speed at the same quality, for users who require extreme generation speed.”
Xiaomi is not traditionally considered one of the six Chinese AI Tigers [ https://substack.com/redirect/cb9a99b5-eb3d-4255-a697-654044950287?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], so it is very surprising to the established order of names you have come to know and love. And… it is natively omnimodal!
Xiaomi made news a few days ago when Fuli Luo, a former DeepSeek star engineer now at Xiaomi [ https://substack.com/redirect/54e5fa0f-ded1-4c18-a610-5ae6f9a0d884?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], started publishing their final RL training runs [ https://substack.com/redirect/b1357b43-7258-490f-a732-18d0aa6e404a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] live, which showed an abnormal amount of transparency in their internal metrics [ https://substack.com/redirect/d4e29302-cc9e-428c-b8a7-38828263a366?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
As they note in their technical report [ https://substack.com/redirect/55eecbd1-f485-4632-812b-27df41106809?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], they scaled RL compute along three axes:
Larger batches and higher throughput: large batches on a fully asynchronous architecture, with 1,568 samples per update, training at up to 1M context length, and 3.5 to 3.7B tokens per step.
More tasks and richer environments: a multi-task training suite spanning coding, general agents, visual and cyber, mixed across several harnesses so that gains in one capability reinforce the others.
More grader compute: relative comparison within each group gives long-horizon RL tasks more precise and more diverse reward signals, closes a self-improvement loop, and steers the model toward shorter paths and fewer tokens per task.
ALL of this tooling, including the environments, will be open sourced.- the environment code and training recipes, but the complete 7k+ task datasets have not yet been released.

Open Models, Competition, and the China Gap
Open models remain the central policy and market story: Nathan Lambert [ https://substack.com/redirect/0f87ecef-60ff-4ea6-8fc5-ac45d9636aa6?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] shared a congressional briefing on open-model performance, adoption, and U.S.-China competition, followed by a public summary [ https://substack.com/redirect/21542a67-0c96-4c6b-bc02-f0675be62d9d?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. The broader argument resurfaced elsewhere: @Yuchenj_UW [ https://substack.com/redirect/13a606dc-2572-4baa-beb1-e99a91bb729b?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] claims frontier coding capability has plateaued since Opus 4.8, while open-source models keep closing the gap at 10–50x lower cost; @ClementDelangue [ https://substack.com/redirect/c6b01279-d643-4a33-ba64-f765bf3d01ff?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] similarly argues APIs are overkill for many real-world use cases and that specialized models will take share. Counterpoint: @teortaxesTex [ https://substack.com/redirect/03391d7e-fffc-439b-a2a5-3daf43e5b0bb?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argues frontier has actually split into new higher tiers, with internal models and top closed models still well ahead.
The release cadence from Chinese labs is now difficult to dismiss: @Thom_Wolf [ https://substack.com/redirect/06c791ec-5d30-4fb1-8313-4e4f30851a35?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] compiled an unusually dense ~10-week run of open releases including Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-Max, DeepSeek V4-Pro, GLM-5.3, Hy4 Preview, Atria Dawn, and more. This is reinforced by a Bloomberg-sourced note via @Polymarket [ https://substack.com/redirect/128f3c63-4b26-4a2b-a8e7-6ea65159e6b5?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] that startups are increasingly building custom models on open weights to cut cost and reduce dependence on OpenAI/Anthropic. The subtext across several tweets: open-weight capability is no longer confined to midsized models; multiple teams are shipping frontier-scale MoEs with credible cost-performance stories.
Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6 and RL as the New Scaling Lever
MiMo-V2.6 is the biggest open-model release in the set: @XiaomiMiMo [ https://substack.com/redirect/8a86a6b5-fcda-4e5b-aa16-58321a72cd84?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] launched MiMo-V2.6 Pro and Flash, described as open omnimodal models with weights, technical report, RL environments, and training code. Artificial Analysis [ https://substack.com/redirect/47c09434-4210-4959-ac3b-831045457f5a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] says MiMo-V2.6-Pro debuts as the top open-weights model on its Intelligence Index (46), with 1.02T total / 42B active parameters and strong cost efficiency at $0.435/M input and $0.87/M output tokens. @victormustar [ https://substack.com/redirect/1dcf9991-d409-4c6c-969d-09d0612e25c0?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] notes the models are under MIT license.
What stood out technically was not just the model, but the RL stack: @eliebakouch [ https://substack.com/redirect/12299e69-0353-470f-861e-5c91942efcda?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted Xiaomi’s environment/data-factory paper for generating RL tasks from open repositories with “agents in the loop” for robustness and anti-cheating. Later commentary points to a second paper and unusually high transparency: @xeophon [ https://substack.com/redirect/f4396092-38e9-4b8a-b7ba-7bfc62543c07?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] notes Xiaomi wants to release ~7K RL environments, and @eliebakouch [ https://substack.com/redirect/79f3707d-3e64-4de7-99e4-613e42c92419?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] emphasizes the team shipped model + tech report less than a week after the final RL run. A recurring interpretation, from @bertgodel [ https://substack.com/redirect/c8b04d81-4e4f-4a7a-a7bd-5f730f3b9d2f?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] and @Thom_Wolf [ https://substack.com/redirect/6c1cc9ea-222d-40ec-b528-53469e3f5798?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], is that high-quality open RL environments may now be as strategically important as pretraining corpora were in the last cycle.
RL cost/throughput details drew attention because they compress timelines: @zephyr_z9 [ https://substack.com/redirect/687425b6-14e7-45d7-b6b3-220abea1da00?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] cites 130 hours, 75B tokens, and $2.6M for the RL run behind the result; @tianjun_zhang [ https://substack.com/redirect/1bf297f2-754d-4b11-bbd3-fb65fc2d9750?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] says the MiMo family scales RL on JAX + TPU, where scaling is “mostly a config change, not a code rewrite.” If these numbers hold up, the implication is that post-training/RL is becoming a far cheaper route to frontier-adjacent gains than many assumed.
Decision Models, Jev, and the Return of Specialized Inference
Jev was the dominant product/theme discussion: Multiple posts converged on the same framing: this is “just” classification/routing, but with modern model intelligence and much better latency/cost. @karpathy [ https://substack.com/redirect/2cd5c607-23c3-4b8a-b4b6-05d2db786187?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] calls it a point on the Pareto frontier for “no thinking, single token, low latency acceptable intelligence”. @willdepue [ https://substack.com/redirect/73bc0e8a-d7ff-4b9c-b77f-7fa938b78cea?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] describes it as a zero-shot classifier with frontier-ish intelligence, while @ClementDelangue [ https://substack.com/redirect/8434753c-8ae0-43db-89a6-19f3958f164c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argues the excitement shows there is large latent demand for specialized models rather than ever-larger generalists.
The ecosystem around Jev expanded quickly: @sarah_edo [ https://substack.com/redirect/f2e1d957-f7b4-405b-92f4-177892f5ff62?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] built a Chrome extension that uses Jev to select and fill relevant WebMCP tools per keystroke. LangChain [ https://substack.com/redirect/6149dee3-6a25-47b8-b523-bfb1665a50cf?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] added Jev-as-a-judge to LangSmith; @hwchase17 [ https://substack.com/redirect/a7f90bf6-94a0-4e12-80af-e7cb13341b72?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] and @Hacubu [ https://substack.com/redirect/6b87d559-9883-42d6-9f3e-b12af7509d07?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] pushed SemIf, an open-source decision model, through the LangSmith Gateway. @omarsar0 [ https://substack.com/redirect/8dd24c64-7c18-4ef5-b36f-3b58e62ad57c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] reports using Jev to retag ~2.3K papers in 83 seconds for $0.14, with 579 high-confidence changes and manual validation of disagreements.
The more durable takeaway is architectural: DSPyOSS [ https://substack.com/redirect/4e602479-9233-4063-aa91-ccd0fa949805?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argues that asking frontier agents is like managing people, while hand-writing decision-model programs is analogous to writing assembly; both extremes are useful, but brittle if overused. Several posts emphasized where these models fit best: routing, approval gates, trace scoring, tool selection, discrete document decisions, and low-cost supervision inside larger agent loops rather than as standalone “smart agents.”
Inference, Tooling, and Systems Optimizations
Tokenizer and post-training infra both got substantive upgrades: Hugging Face’s tokenizers v1 RC [ https://substack.com/redirect/2ba730de-e76a-4ffc-8e78-b4b00b14ae1e?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] claims up to 30x faster tokenization, improved multithread scaling, lower memory use, and much smaller package size; @art_zucker [ https://substack.com/redirect/b5f5cd19-61b9-472e-b982-efb62ffeea27?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] framed it as a new SOTA tokenization library. Separately, Halo [ https://substack.com/redirect/29ce4017-afa6-41e4-b10e-ecd9d6ff4e60?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] launched as a post-training framework claiming up to 2.8x throughput over stock TRL while keeping models in native Hugging Face format.
Inference-side engineering remains a major lever: @RisingSayak [ https://substack.com/redirect/a9aac665-5846-4245-af48-27454e6fa6ed?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] showed how KV caching is incorporated into QwenImage 2.1, separating fixed context from changing image positions and yielding a 2.55x speedup; the thread cites 50.57s → 19.86s DiT time on a warmed A100 with moderate memory overhead. vLLM [ https://substack.com/redirect/a8a35432-b7f9-4c17-b08e-4059d19cd6e8?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] published tuned serving configs for Qwen3.8-2.4T on GB300 NVL72, showing a Pareto frontier from 5K total tok/s/GPU at high throughput to 180 output tok/s/user at low latency. In video workloads, vLLM [ https://substack.com/redirect/0751775d-276e-48c3-a519-e4d84759d8e6?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] also integrated PyNvVideoCodec/NVDEC, removing CPU decode bottlenecks and reporting 2x+ throughput at 8×H100.
Compression/quantization is still moving fast: @ZhihuFrontier [ https://substack.com/redirect/75ff7763-3346-4d12-981d-fe1a0c15ad5d?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] summarized Tencent Hunyuan’s engineering behind packing Hy4 Preview (770B) into 214 GiB via mixed-precision quantization averaging ~2.38 bits/weight, including custom CUDA kernels in patched llama.cpp. On the edge/local side, @vikhyatk [ https://substack.com/redirect/23ff6e09-079f-4ebc-8f85-ebf84f35edd4?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] released Parakeet Redux, compressing NVIDIA’s speech model from 1.2GB to 178MB, running at 113x realtime on CPU, while beating the base model on 25-language FLEURS and staying within 0.3 WER on English.
Agents, Security, and Human-in-the-Loop Control
Computer-use systems are becoming more productionized, but security is now central: Patrick Wardle [ https://substack.com/redirect/c4748259-d3af-42dc-9942-5025e1360952?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] reported a serious local-hijack flaw in Muse, arguing broad OS access makes such assistants a high-value attack surface. In contrast, DeepLearningAI [ https://substack.com/redirect/1c96048f-78b8-4601-bb15-1674a3a57c14?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted Meta’s design philosophy for Muse-like agents: assume prompt injection will happen, keep real credentials away from the model, isolate tools in containers, and use an independent outbound-call gatekeeper.
Commercial agents are also being pushed deeper into workflows: Cognition [ https://substack.com/redirect/2d4c416c-f23d-4e3e-aadd-b14e7e6f85c5?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] introduced Devin Cloud in Terminal and devin ssh, making the model’s VM directly accessible from the CLI and allowing handoff between Devin and the user’s machine. GitHub Copilot [ https://substack.com/redirect/0c6b2965-d13f-4280-bf76-3a757b43dc19?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] teased editable diffs in the desktop app, while @pierceboggan [ https://substack.com/redirect/3006c32c-7002-4b81-a528-5b9935c983d8?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] showed a Sentry-integrated canvas for moving from crash report to fix.
A recurring systems point: inference and agent infra are shifting toward test-time compute: @sarahookr [ https://substack.com/redirect/1dc374b9-1d35-4f23-8831-ae74a5a86634?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] predicts compute moving from pretraining—where marginal FLOPs yield less—to test-time compute, requiring “very different infrastructure.” That theme also showed up in persistent-cache discussions for local serving, e.g. @TheZachMueller [ https://substack.com/redirect/2e9046c1-5292-4a0b-8a71-97aa82ca213c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] on SGLang’s multi-level hiCache (GPU/RAM/disk) for preserving KV cache across model swaps and restarts.

1. Qwen-Image 2.1 and Tiny Open Image Models
Qwen-Image-2.1 released! [ https://substack.com/redirect/f43e540e-a210-4d52-ae1e-899cb17b6da3?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] (Activity: 2485): Qwen-Image-2.1 was released with open weights as a unified 7B image generation/editing model, positioned as a faster, lower-cost member of the Qwen-Image series (blog [ https://substack.com/redirect/024622f8-9d89-4c37-9e8e-fd22d85cbe85?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], GitHub [ https://substack.com/redirect/191064a1-2da6-42dc-ae8e-eac6c4d1d58c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], Hugging Face [ https://substack.com/redirect/4543924f-1db5-4e47-bf93-f3569c69903a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]). Key technical additions include native RGBA/transparent image generation and editing, support for up to 10 reference images, multi-image inference acceleration, and localized edit control for tasks like object removal, attribute changes, product/portrait-preserving edits, panoramas, infographics, typography, and virtual try-ons. Comments primarily highlight the native transparency pipeline and local-edit interface; one example uses colored circles to target three regions simultaneously for removal, hair recoloring, and clothing replacement, suggesting interest in more controllable multi-region editing workflows.
Qwen-Image-2.1 is reported to add native transparent image generation and transparent-image editing support, which is technically notable because alpha-channel workflows are often handled as post-processing or masking rather than directly by the image model. The linked example shows transparent-output capability: https://preview.redd.it/59fu834idoqh1.png?width=767&format=png&auto=webp&s=5fb81b135b35dac70f9d38a9995d7c1a7a2877dd [ https://substack.com/redirect/71ddf520-7980-40c7-93b9-02da01514899?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]
The model appears to support multi-region local editing via visual annotations, where circled regions can be referenced in the prompt and edited simultaneously. One example asks it to “remove the metal watch in the blue circle, change the hair in the red circle to black, and replace the area in the green circle with gray short-sleeved linen pajamas,” demonstrating combined object removal, attribute modification, and region replacement in a single edit pass: https://preview.redd.it/cvh09tyvdoqh1.jpeg?width=1242&format=pjpg&auto=webp&s=32077f7420def5bec85160e2e982d6aef5efce54 [ https://substack.com/redirect/2f385d68-f1aa-4b20-9e8d-d3601dd1d314?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]
Several commenters highlight the model size: Qwen-Image-2.1 is described as 7B parameters, which is significantly smaller than prior Qwen image models that commenters say were over 20B. This size reduction is viewed as important for local inference feasibility, with one user specifically noting interest from the perspective of a 16GB VRAM GPU such as the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB.
Clarification on the Qwen-image-2.1 license [ https://substack.com/redirect/5f27c407-3082-47c4-9212-117b184bdddd?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] (Activity: 948): The image is a non-meme screenshot of a Qwen Developers X post [ https://substack.com/redirect/626bde87-43db-43bf-8c79-42542405ac25?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] clarifying that Qwen-Image-2.1 outputs are not considered licensed “Materials”, so users retain rights to generated images/content. This matters because the model license reportedly still contains a non-commercial restriction on use of the Materials, creating ambiguity over whether commercial image generation is allowed even if generated outputs are user-owned. Commenters welcomed the clarification, with one user saying Qwen-Image-2.1 “easily beats all current Flux models.” Another noted they can run it locally via ComfyUI int8 on a 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti peaking around 15.2 GB VRAM, but warned the Hugging Face LICENSE file may not yet reflect the clarified intent.
A commenter reports running Qwen-Image-2.1 locally in ComfyUI using int8 quantization on a 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti, with VRAM peaking around 15.2 GB. They describe the model as suitable for local testing but note that licensing uncertainty around generated outputs was the main blocker for broader/client use.
Several commenters highlight a legal/implementation mismatch: the Hugging Face README was apparently clarified, but the actual LICENSE file still contains Section 2(b) language prohibiting commercial “use” of the Materials. One user emailed model-business@notice.qwencloud.com asking whether the license text will be updated, because the tweet/README intent may not be sufficient for client or commercial work.
The key technical/legal distinction being debated is whether “commercial use not allowed” applies only to serving, redistributing, or monetizing the model/materials, versus also restricting outputs generated by the model. Commenters argue that until the canonical license file is updated, downstream users comparing it with permissive Apache-2.0/MIT-style model licenses may reasonably avoid commercial workflows despite the clarification...
