**1. AI executives confirm AI demand shows no sign of slowing, even as the race pivots to value:** Companies are starting to get more cautious about how much they spend on AI, but that hasn’t slowed demand. Former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/12/ai-demand-chips-data-centers-stock-volatility.html?utm_campaign=ai-demand-valuemaxxing&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=superhuman) that AI demand is "almost unlimited,” while Marc Boroditsky, CRO of AI infrastructure firm Nebius, echoed the point, saying there’s “much more demand than we’re able to fill.”

**2. Anthropic lands another high-profile hire:** Tom Blomfield [announced](https://x.com/t_blom/status/2076580921398931788?s=20) he’s leaving Y Combinator to join Anthropic’s AI compute team. It’s the latest notable pickup for Anthropic, with Blomfield joining Nobel laureate John Jumper, OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy, UC Berkeley professor Jelani Nelson, and former Federal Reserve Chairman Ben Bernanke, who [joined](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-ai-oversight-board-adds-202229682.html) the lab’s independent oversight board.

**3. TSMC's June revenue rockets 68% as the AI-chip boom rolls on:** The world's largest contract chipmaker [posted](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/tsmc-june-revenue-rises-percent-ahead-second-quarter.html) its highest-ever monthly revenue in June, ending its second quarter at $39.6 billion. The strong results signal that demand for AI components is still tight. TSMC is sold out of N3, the process node behind this year's leading AI GPUs and CPUs, and is adding two new plants in southern Taiwan to keep up with demand.

##### **FROM THE FRONTIER**

## **AI pushback is reaching a breaking point as 200+ economists demand change**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/cc601cb5-e254-44e7-aa67-381980239a26/superhumanteam_A_robot_taking_questions_at_a_city_hall_event__58eb1343-db3c-41d1-90af-3edccc22adc8_0.jpg?t=1783969922)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**Demanding change. **Over** **200 economists, including 16 Nobel laureates, have signed “[We Must Act Now](https://www.wemustactnow.ai/),” a statement urging governments to steer AI in a direction that benefits humanity broadly. The public seems to agree: a [recent Verasight survey](https://reports.verasight.io/reports/june-2026-ai-survey#key-takeaways) found that 60% of respondents feel anxious about AI’s rise, with that same survey surfacing several popular solutions.

**Popular reforms.** Verasight’s respondents want oversight. 89% of respondents support requiring frontier labs to publicly disclose safety-testing results, and 81% want the government to block potentially dangerous models before release. Another idea gaining traction? A sovereign wealth fund. 69% favored forcing AI firms to hand over equity stakes that would distribute AI's gains broadly.

**How would it work?** In theory, a fund would fill [three main roles](https://windfalltrust.org/policy-atlas/sovereign-wealth-funds): steering AI development at the national level, taking equity stakes in new companies, and converting private-sector wealth creation into public revenue to fund social safety nets. At least one AI lab isn’t opposed to the idea.

**The reforms already taking shape. **OpenAI [already proposed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposes-us-government-own-5percent-stake-to-address-political-blowback.html) letting the US government take a 5% stake earlier this month, and the US government [reviewed](https://www.engadget.com/2205599/anthropic-redeploy-mythos-fable-ai-models/) both Anthropic and OpenAI’s most powerful models prior to release. The gap between what people want and what’s happening is starting to close — but expect this issue to continue escalating over the coming months.
