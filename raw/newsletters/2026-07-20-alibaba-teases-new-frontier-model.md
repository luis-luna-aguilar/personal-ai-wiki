**1. China's AI labs keep closing the gap:** Alibaba just put Qwen3.8, its next flagship model, into [live preview](https://www.qwencloud.com/pricing/token-plan), claiming it rivals the best from OpenAI and Anthropic and trails only Anthropic's Fable 5. It arrives just days after Moonshot AI shipped Kimi K3, a reminder of how quickly Chinese frontier labs are catching up.

**2. China outlines its global vision for AI: **At Shanghai's World AI Conference, China's President Xi [called](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/chinas-xi-calls-for-more-global-efforts-to-guide-ai-chides-us-for-its-curbs-on-tech-sharing) on countries to build artificial intelligence together, arguing the technology should not be dominated by any single nation. He also outlined a plan to offer 5,000 AI training spots to developing countries. The push signals a growing global contest over who sets the rules for AI, which could shape everything from the tools you use to how they are regulated.

**3. Decart brings special effects to the live stream:** The AI lab's most advanced model yet, Lucy 2.5, [edits videos in real time](https://decart.ai/lucy) with barely any lag, letting you add or remove objects, restyle the frame, and conjure effects on the fly. Decart sees it powering ecommerce, streaming, and advertising. For viewers, it hints at a near future where the polished visuals you usually see in finished films could show up in a live broadcast.
