## **How to navigate the “AI fog” that’s making long-term planning much harder**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/1601fec9-a6a7-4dad-ac98-0cb581309188/superhumanteam_A_robot_trying_to_see_through_fog_--ar_169_--s_92ccaa9f-ea6a-44a6-b0ff-ac318561790a_0.jpg?t=1778080893)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**The AI fog.** Feeling unusually uncertain about the future? Harvard Business Review has a new name for that: [AI fog](https://hbr.org/2026/04/the-future-is-shrouded-in-an-ai-fog). UC Berkeley-Haas professor Toby Stuart argues AI is rewriting the future faster than we can plan for it — making it harder to be confident in our decisions.

**Rethinking long bets. **Life runs on long-term commitments: 4-year degrees, 30-year mortgages, decades-long career arcs. Each one assumes that tomorrow will be a slightly upgraded version of today, so the investment you make now will pay off in the future. AI is breaking that assumption.

**Reshaping decisions.** Is climbing the corporate ladder still worth it if AI automates your dream role before you get there? Will an advanced degree still be relevant by the time you graduate? Should you sign a lease or mortgage when the job market might look completely different in five years? That’s AI fog in action — not pessimism, just a sudden inability to underwrite the future.

**So, what should you do?** HBR suggests a mentality shift: swap long-term planning and optimize for optionality instead. Reskill often, pivot to new opportunities as they emerge, and be mentally prepared to walk away from existing plans — even if you’ve invested heavily in them. When the future is uncertain, keeping your options open may be a better strategy than locking yourself into any single path.
