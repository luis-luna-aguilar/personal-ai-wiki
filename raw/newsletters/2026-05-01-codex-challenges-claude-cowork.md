## **AI-generated music is transitioning from fad to full-blown industry**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/9aca56db-b07c-462c-91d4-be410fa19e3b/superhumanteam_A_robot_singing_on_stage_--ar_169_--raw_--sref_fdf67fc6-3735-44b4-b65b-9d75fcdb0845_1__1_.jpg?t=1777571785)
Caption: 


--------------------
**Mic’d up. **AI music felt like it might be a passing novelty that would quickly fade. It hasn’t. ElevenLabs, the $11B voice generation lab, just launched [ElevenMusic](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/introducing-elevenmusic), a platform where users can create and discover AI tracks. It joins Suno and Udio in a category that's gone from fad to full-blown industry in roughly a year.

**Flooding the charts.** Last November, Breaking Rust [became](https://www.newsweek.com/breaking-rust-ai-music-country-digital-sales-11022040) the first fully AI artist to hit #1 on a Billboard chart. Weeks later, Telisha Jones used Suno to create Xania Monet, another AI chart-topper that landed a $3M deal. Suno itself just [crossed](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7432540569316364289/) $300M ARR with 2M paid subscribers — many of them working on the next AI hit.

**Unlocking creativity.** AI tools eliminate the need for an expensive studio, years of practice, and knowledge of music theory. The result: anyone with a laptop can create music that (based on the number of streams some AI songs have gotten) genuinely resonates with audiences. But there’s a fine line between democratization and extraction.

**Protecting artists.** In a world where anyone can create a chart-topper from a text prompt, the artists who actually inspired the training data deserve recognition and protection. Expect rightsholder economics to become a key issue over the next 12 months as AI song streams — and revenue — continue to soar.

## **Design Your Deep Work Environment**

```
Prompt: 
Act as a deep work environment architect applying Nikola Tesla's isolation principles — Tesla built dedicated laboratories specifically engineered to eliminate every distraction and signal to his brain that entering meant one thing only: intense creative work.

Design a physical and digital environment that signals deep work the moment I enter it — making distraction structurally impossible and focus structurally inevitable.

 
1. Ask for my current workspace setup, available space, and biggest environmental distractions before starting
2. Design the physical environment — layout, lighting, sound, and visual cues that trigger focus
3. Design the digital environment — apps closed, notifications off, and tools ready before the session starts
4. Create an environment entry ritual — the 2-minute sequence that tells my brain deep work begins now
5. Build a distraction elimination system — making every interruption harder to access than the work



- Physical and digital environment must be set up before the session — never during
- Entry ritual must be under 2 minutes — never elaborate enough to skip
- Every distraction must have added friction — not just willpower
- Environment must work even on zero motivation days
- Test: would Tesla recognize this as a place where serious work happens


Physical Environment Design → Digital Environment Design → Entry Ritual → Distraction Elimination System
```
