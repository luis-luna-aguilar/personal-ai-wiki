Coding Models, Agent Harnesses, and the Fable 5 Re-launch
Anthropic re-enabled Claude Fable 5, but with visible safety fallbacks: After a day of pent-up demand, @claudeai [ https://substack.com/redirect/7403b5a2-7115-4514-9587-31316bd2db2b?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] announced Fable 5 is back, alongside a clarifying note that updated cybersecurity safeguards may route some requests to Opus 4.8, with biology/chemistry classifiers still overly broad for now @claudeai [ https://substack.com/redirect/aa343fdb-13f4-4f29-a959-b765e93badd6?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. The relaunch immediately propagated into tooling: Cursor says Fable 5 leads its evals but is the most expensive per task @cursor_ai [ https://substack.com/redirect/381e80cf-e96e-41b7-b333-07a962125444?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]; Devin added it across Cloud/Desktop/CLI @cognition [ https://substack.com/redirect/7ec41c9d-d35c-411b-9abd-2275da1d7207?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]; Perplexity restored it as an orchestrator model @perplexity_ai [ https://substack.com/redirect/20b0b8ad-01c1-4e73-a2fc-640e5db06e92?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. Anthropic also reset rate limits for users once the model was live again @ClaudeDevs [ https://substack.com/redirect/e3a9bf99-fe7d-4973-ad42-9da351807bc0?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
The interesting story was less “model is back” than “how people are adapting to frontier-model constraints”: Multiple builders converged on multi-model orchestration rather than single-model dependence. @theo [ https://substack.com/redirect/2be080bb-4e15-499e-a94e-ee3829bbc495?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] described using Fable only for higher-value reasoning/planning while delegating implementation, verification, and computer-use work to other models; he reports a substantial improvement in end-to-end PR yield @theo [ https://substack.com/redirect/aff4a5cb-0c20-49ee-9dc3-911aeebc9a8b?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. Similar views came from @omarsar0 [ https://substack.com/redirect/b558151d-37f6-41b0-8c60-3dfbade62c5c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], who argued teams should design model-combination strategies rather than build around one frontier model, and from @MParakhin [ https://substack.com/redirect/62c4aa8f-3e58-4a27-a015-687464ec696a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], who pushed back on “simple-task pre-classifiers,” arguing that reliable routing often requires solving the task first. On the benchmark side, @kimmonismus [ https://substack.com/redirect/aa212fc0-e327-41d7-9f22-a935c6170fbb?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted Fable 5’s 16.10% on the Remote Labor Index, while @ArtificialAnlys [ https://substack.com/redirect/0b9cd28f-c9b6-426f-bfa1-5ea743a8fc75?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] reported Sonnet 5 ranking second on AA-Briefcase but with much higher turn counts and weaker cost-performance tradeoffs at lower effort settings.
Open Models, Chinese Labs, and the Expanding Coding Stack Around GLM-5.2
Z.ai is building product surface area around GLM-5.2, not just shipping a checkpoint: The most concrete launch was ZCode, the official dev environment for GLM-5.2, with BYOK support, cross-platform availability, and a quota boost for coding-plan subscribers @Zai_org [ https://substack.com/redirect/a112ee96-fa7e-428d-90ef-bbae310ec417?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. Commentary from @kimmonismus [ https://substack.com/redirect/6cd50e32-3887-4a8b-9ec2-52bd467525e2?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] framed it as an AI-native coding IDE optimized for GLM workflows and long-running autonomous tasks. The surrounding ecosystem is moving quickly too: LangChain published guides for using GLM-5.2 in coding flows @LangChain [ https://substack.com/redirect/6b1c0595-bf7b-49ff-ad34-52c350f5f271?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], and @hwchase17 [ https://substack.com/redirect/2ac5b576-7668-4047-a81a-9e60c524b273?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] explicitly called out developers turning to GLM-5.2 as a daily driver.
Benchmarks suggest open coding models are closing specific gaps even if not leading overall frontier performance: @mercor_ai [ https://substack.com/redirect/b0605af4-61bd-44e1-873a-52b04d4ac6ba?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] reported GLM 5.2 as the first open model to lead a category on APEX-SWE, posting 55.3% Pass@1 on Integration, and ranking as the best open model tested overall there; Kimi K2.7 followed closely. That complements @scaling01 [ https://substack.com/redirect/4e2a861b-7f2a-44c9-a3b5-05cd1c9866fc?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], who cautioned against overclaiming that GLM has surpassed top Western frontier models while still acknowledging a rapidly shrinking coding gap.
Inference work around open models is becoming a meaningful part of the story: @vllm_project [ https://substack.com/redirect/f3ddb55a-a5ee-4e34-95d1-9f65f519a526?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] landed native DSpark speculative decoding support in vLLM for DeepSeek models, reporting around 250 tok/s on 8×B300 with improved acceptance over MTP, and @mgoin_ [ https://substack.com/redirect/a6112ecc-ac7d-41c7-b868-2333cc267227?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] released a GLM-5.2 DSpark preview claiming roughly 1.5× faster decode. Separately, @jon_durbin [ https://substack.com/redirect/3d6db72b-ca41-401e-a8aa-e6a20765fa4a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] reported an in-house dflash drafter on Qwen3-32B yielding ~50% higher throughput on the same hardware.
Agent Infrastructure: Memory, Wikis, Skill Composition, and Structured Workflows
“Wiki memory” is emerging as a practical design pattern for agents: @sydneyrunkle [ https://substack.com/redirect/4ccbf2c5-fad9-4089-bac4-a4cc9165d635?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argued for wiki-structured memory as a simple, extensible substrate, and that idea rapidly turned into product releases. LangChain launched OpenWiki, a tool to generate and maintain agent-consumable codebase docs with openwiki --init @BraceSproul [ https://substack.com/redirect/c3d24f30-1d63-4e51-892f-29e6cdbd736c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], @LangChain [ https://substack.com/redirect/09557729-d6ae-4bf0-be5e-807fc5764e21?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. The motivation is consistent across posts: agents repeatedly lose working context between threads and need a maintained, inspectable knowledge layer rather than raw logs @caspar_br [ https://substack.com/redirect/da2c9874-6ad0-4f00-a265-df3c3788fe8d?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
Memory systems are shifting from retrieval-only to reconciliation and maintenance: Weaviate’s Engram pitch is representative here: candidate memories are extracted, transformed against existing memory, and only then committed, so contradictions are resolved once rather than at every query @PrajjwalYd [ https://substack.com/redirect/27ea9051-a033-45ff-be9e-323e881035a5?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. @bpalit [ https://substack.com/redirect/c4708fa9-f3c1-4902-81c0-2cfacf209058?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] extends the same argument to enterprise settings, where agent memory must be governed, permission-aware, and shared—not just a folder of markdown files.
Structured composition is replacing naive “give the model all the tools” approaches: @omarsar0 [ https://substack.com/redirect/37291cee-538c-45be-be02-47706af62ef1?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted SkillComposer, which treats skill selection as a joint autoregressive composition problem and reports +23.1pp / +18.2pp gains on SkillsBench over no-skill baselines. On the framework side, Deep Agents added support for recursive language model workflows @sydneyrunkle [ https://substack.com/redirect/ae8293f5-1e87-4103-b47c-cd7806b8da7e?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], and @hwchase17 [ https://substack.com/redirect/956ddf1a-be20-4b3b-9504-177e49c7af55?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] connected dynamic subagents to patterns like Agentic MapReduce. This general direction—more explicit workflow structure, fan-out/fan-in patterns, and code-enforced orchestration—showed up repeatedly across products and benchmarks.
Security, Evaluation, and Agentic MapReduce
Cognition’s Devin Security Swarm is one of the clearer examples of agent architecture specializing around a real enterprise workflow: The system uses Agentic MapReduce to fan out bounded agents across a codebase, aggregate findings, and validate exploitability before surfacing confirmed vulnerabilities @cognition [ https://substack.com/redirect/a7620f8a-424d-45b5-8155-30a9969a6cb0?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. Cognition claims this is both more cost-effective and more accurate than alternatives, and says a Fortune 500 pilot found and fixed over a thousand vulnerabilities in production repos @walden_yan [ https://substack.com/redirect/a0034754-8142-4edb-a22a-e279fa4da9f6?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. The broader reaction from builders like @jakejluo [ https://substack.com/redirect/2c51e702-566c-4eea-a406-1c4c7d76d5c3?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] and @levie [ https://substack.com/redirect/f229054c-4dc7-4386-821e-6a859b1339d7?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] was that this pattern will generalize to large-scale document, code, and knowledge workflows.
AI-agent evaluation is quickly becoming its own subfield: @random_walker [ https://substack.com/redirect/c84fc758-7929-4149-932b-e3e0f6fcf41c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] noted several new papers advancing agent evaluation and described it as a distinct discipline. Practical examples included Agent Arena re-enabling Fable 5 in agent mode @arena [ https://substack.com/redirect/0d1f60ae-e1c4-4b0a-af7a-45b18fb84671?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], AA-AgentPerf for agents-per-megawatt system benchmarking @ArtificialAnlys [ https://substack.com/redirect/bdce3029-fb03-4247-8cc2-69c26b238c85?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], and WorldModelGym, which evaluates whether a world model actually supports good decision-making rather than just producing plausible simulations @RekaAILabs [ https://substack.com/redirect/92a5af7f-77a9-4a7b-9012-dd7f85ecb134?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
There is also a push toward better reporting pipelines for AI failures: FLARE-AI, launched with a coalition spanning cyber and AI safety researchers, aims to standardize flaw and incident reporting so issues can be routed to the right developers and registries instead of disappearing into siloed intake forms @ClementDelangue [ https://substack.com/redirect/899fecef-9650-402a-9d19-b9017dd24b91?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], @ShayneRedford [ https://substack.com/redirect/25bcf5c9-9cdc-45ce-a1a4-c72611f602e0?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
Systems, Inference, and Architecture Work Worth Watching
NVIDIA’s TwoTower result stands out as a concrete speed/quality tradeoff on generation architecture: @NVIDIAAI [ https://substack.com/redirect/41a403ff-0201-4538-93c7-4e500aaa0140?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] introduced Nemotron-Labs-TwoTower, adapting a 30B model into a diffusion-style language model that writes tokens in parallel via a two-copy setup. Claimed result: 2.42× faster generation while preserving 98.7% of the original model’s quality. @LiorOnAI [ https://substack.com/redirect/fa69711d-9d4f-4e2e-97a9-146c9459ff66?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] summarized the trick as reusing a frozen context model plus a trained writer model, avoiding full retraining from scratch.
On-device and browser inference continue to benefit from agentic optimization and specialized runtimes: @googlegemma [ https://substack.com/redirect/049bbc42-322d-4528-9066-855e438cf976?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted WebGPU Gemma 4 running at 255 tok/s on M4, attributed to kernels written with Fable 5. @andimarafioti [ https://substack.com/redirect/f8298ce2-9dd4-4aa1-82db-79eab58338fe?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] demoed a fully open-source realtime voice stack around Gemma 4 31B with Cerebras inference, aiming as a drop-in alternative to OpenAI’s realtime API. At the kernel level, Hugging Face’s kernels library now exposes MiniMax’s MSA kernel @RisingSayak [ https://substack.com/redirect/4970a8af-2b4c-4298-9be8-017a7cedae31?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], and Triton-on-Mac drew interest as well @QuixiAI [ https://substack.com/redirect/bb03af57-9ba4-4b6d-832f-1f74b69a393e?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
Architecture research beyond vanilla LLM scaling also surfaced: @gklambauer [ https://substack.com/redirect/4481b6ba-cb4e-40b9-98ed-84a6b6d70962?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] pointed to AdaJEPA, a LeCun-led world-model approach with test-time adaptation via latent-state prediction error; @LiorOnAI [ https://substack.com/redirect/d57c66ca-7fe8-4372-8a7c-75e5eb0297ad?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] summarized NEO as learning reusable causal “programs” rather than only next-frame prediction; and @ziv_ravid [ https://substack.com/redirect/ebe0eeee-6ce9-478c-97d4-3fcb41cc5e9d?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted “training in imagination” as an active paradigm rather than just speculation.
