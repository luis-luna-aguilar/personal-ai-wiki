There are a number of official benchmarks that the team shows beating both GPT-Realtime-2 [ https://substack.com/redirect/4bebd86e-e53c-439e-907a-d9c292e5b73d?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] and Gemini 3.1-Flash [ https://substack.com/redirect/7701c7b6-2f95-486e-b211-06a86f56fd14?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] on basic things like BigBench Audio and IFEval and FD-bench, but the level of interactivity aimed for required making 2 new internal benchmarks for time awareness, simultaneous translation, and visual proactivity:
TimeSpeak: Can the model initiate speech at user-specified times? 
Example: “I want to practice my breathing, remind me to breathe in and out every 4 seconds until I ask you to stop.”
CueSpeak: Can the model speak at the appropriate moment? 
Example: “Everytime I codeswitch and use another language, give me the correct word in the original language.”
RepCount-A [ https://substack.com/redirect/8b78cd16-92a2-4d4d-93f9-2182716c8a4d?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] contains videos of repeated actions and is adapted into an online counting task - measures continuous visual tracking and timely counting.
ProactiveVideoQA [ https://substack.com/redirect/4b4d8b62-eb8e-4e0c-a0ee-05028595a444?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] consists of videos with questions, whose answers become available at specific moments. Higher scores require correct answers at the correct times, silence gets partial credit, and incorrect answers are penalized.
Charades [ https://substack.com/redirect/bd2724c3-a02f-43ae-bd6a-25232502761b?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] is a standard temporal action-localization benchmark.

AI Twitter Recap
Thinking Machines’ Native Interaction Models and the Shift Beyond Turn-Based AI
Full-duplex multimodal interaction as a first-class model capability: The day’s clearest technical theme was Thinking Machines’ preview of “interaction models” [ https://substack.com/redirect/01f838b3-a3ba-46b6-a517-534d13ca63f6?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], described as models trained from scratch for real-time interaction rather than layering speech, turn-taking, and tool use onto a turn-based LLM. The accompanying technical post [ https://substack.com/redirect/b6238426-d315-450b-8ccc-e2ae5ea4e7b0?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] and team commentary from @johnschulman2 [ https://substack.com/redirect/4678af04-bf8e-4bdd-af16-5461e036fc11?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], @soumithchintala [ https://substack.com/redirect/435e7e76-9ed1-4ecc-bc35-f36c3d3ab487?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], and @cHHillee [ https://substack.com/redirect/0c3a451a-0557-4c2f-9fff-8b50e7beea09?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] frame this as a human↔AI bandwidth problem: models should be able to listen, speak, watch, think, search, and react concurrently. Demos emphasized continuous-time awareness, interruption handling, simultaneous speech, visual proactivity, and background tool use without explicit “now I’m thinking / now I’m searching” boundaries. Team members also highlighted that many tasks that previously needed special-purpose systems become zero-shot once the type signature is effectively continuous audio+video+text → audio+text (@johnschulman2 [ https://substack.com/redirect/688d5741-0fc0-4fa9-a168-e339c485e84c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]).
Why it matters technically: Several reactions converged on the same point: this is not “another chatbot demo” but a change in interface assumptions. @liliyu_lili [ https://substack.com/redirect/8de9a03e-12b4-4ac2-967e-547e69d48ca4?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] pointed to visual proactivity (“tell me when I start slouching”, “count my pushups”) as a missing primitive in current systems; @rown [ https://substack.com/redirect/7926256a-ceb9-4ba5-959f-d80d0accd25a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] called it the first general video+speech model that is visually proactive; @kimmonismus [ https://substack.com/redirect/6e0b5039-37b0-4bb0-ad69-a846f35086e5?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] and @giffmana [ https://substack.com/redirect/f5727bd2-8feb-4310-9cd4-9665c2d4cc8b?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] both emphasized that native interactivity is the deeper innovation than raw benchmark claims. This launch also implicitly raises the bar for “realtime” multimodal systems, as noted by @swyx [ https://substack.com/redirect/63d9c473-7627-4d5a-9593-f353cd6bf584?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]. One implementation detail surfaced via @eliebakouch [ https://substack.com/redirect/8b1abee1-d875-4140-9e4d-20768ddc1521?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]: the stack is using SGLang.
OpenAI’s Enterprise and Security Push: Deployment Company and Daybreak
OpenAI is moving down-stack into services and deployment: OpenAI announced the OpenAI Deployment Company [ https://substack.com/redirect/5322b01c-0e60-4813-85a5-be3cc35c28e5?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], a majority-owned unit built to help enterprises deploy frontier models into real workflows. The key operating detail is 150 Forward Deployed Engineers and Deployment Specialists coming in via the acquisition of Tomoro [ https://substack.com/redirect/0f6a8407-067f-465c-887e-73024a966334?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], with @gdb [ https://substack.com/redirect/31479e3e-56dc-4e4c-9189-67f84f01db60?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] citing $4B of initial investment from 19 partners. Multiple observers read this as OpenAI adopting a Palantir-/Microsoft-style field-engineering model: @kimmonismus [ https://substack.com/redirect/f8f64e10-bf9e-45ed-85c6-be3fcbfda1a5?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argued OpenAI wants to own the deployment layer of the AI economy, while @matvelloso [ https://substack.com/redirect/401f209e-b800-4922-88a8-db4603a02796?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] connected it to the historical enterprise success pattern of embedding technical staff close to customer operations.
Daybreak: security-specific model distribution, workflow, and trust tiers: OpenAI also launched Daybreak [ https://substack.com/redirect/8ab91b49-93f9-41b7-b13b-863205cb9633?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], an umbrella effort around defensive cyber operations and continuously securing software, with @sama [ https://substack.com/redirect/aeeef4c3-9b2d-4529-b001-a995602bdb64?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] positioning it as a practical response to rapidly improving AI cyber capability. Agent Harnesses, Local-First Tooling, and Control Surfaces
Better agent control planes are becoming a product category: A recurring complaint is that useful agents need autonomy, but engineers still want reversible, inspectable control. @itsclelia [ https://substack.com/redirect/9e058e0c-ddf2-49f2-802c-8e34e328927f?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] addressed this with aggit, a Rust CLI for local/remote, S3-backed storage of agent artifacts, enabling stash/branch/restore semantics outside the main Git history. In the same vein, @_catwu [ https://substack.com/redirect/ad83d444-4ac5-4981-aff1-5e7101d9e7d4?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted a new claude agents terminal control plane for managing multiple Claude Code agents, and @cursor_ai [ https://substack.com/redirect/a38acef2-3229-46b1-9465-aec41e1cbe08?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] pushed Cursor into Microsoft Teams, where the agent reads the full thread and opens a PR. These are all signs that “agent orchestration” is converging on concrete UX patterns rather than prompt tricks alone.
Deep Agents / Hermes / local agents are maturing quickly: @masondrxy [ https://substack.com/redirect/a897b0be-0a1c-4f2a-b7de-563268fa2781?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] noted that Deep Agents CLI can hot-swap underlying model providers mid-conversation without losing context, a nontrivial systems capability that many agent stacks still miss. LangChain also highlighted harness profiles for provider/model-specific tuning (tweet [ https://substack.com/redirect/06d09efe-4160-4c68-bba7-72b90a5fe44b?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]), and separate pricing analysis from the same author argued that DeepSeek V4 Flash can be dramatically cheaper than GPT/Gemini flash-tier options for high-volume agent workloads (tweet [ https://substack.com/redirect/33156d55-8478-4587-a2a9-b0a606193421?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]). On the local side, Hugging Face added Hermes Agent support in local apps plus native trace visualization [ https://substack.com/redirect/cb4e0e4f-b86d-41d1-9ca1-b2fb41982adf?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], while @Teknium [ https://substack.com/redirect/de81484a-fdba-4942-9a89-bd6d1f4697fd?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] previewed computer use with any model via Hermes Agent and CUA, explicitly targeting local/open models as well as frontier APIs. @onusoz [ https://substack.com/redirect/c7a56558-4ed3-4070-a08f-77d9db0b53c6?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] joining Hugging Face to improve local models in OpenClaw and related open harnesses is another strong signal that local agent ergonomics are now strategic infrastructure.
A design thesis emerging around tools: @threepointone [ https://substack.com/redirect/06a6fb7d-b67d-4d8a-bca3-b10da2135f54?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argued that agents may asymptotically want just two primitive tools: search and execute, with dynamic semantic discovery of capabilities rather than ever-expanding static tool menus. That complements the broader move toward configurable harnesses instead of giant monolithic prompts.
Benchmarks, Efficiency, and Open-Model Economics
Coding-agent benchmarking is finally measuring harness+model pairs: Artificial Analysis launched a Coding Agent Index [ https://substack.com/redirect/e546dc36-c571-48b0-aeaa-09409b8610e8?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] spanning SWE-Bench-Pro-Hard-AA, Terminal-Bench v2, and SWE-Atlas-QnA, comparing not just models but model+harness combinations. Their topline: Opus 4.7 in Cursor CLI scored 61, with GPT-5.5 in Codex/Claude Code close behind; top open-weight setups included GLM-5.1, Kimi K2.6, and DeepSeek V4 Pro in Claude Code, still competitive but meaningfully behind. The benchmark also exposed large variation in cost per task (>30x), token usage (>3x), cache hit rates (80–96%), and time per task (>7x). That benchmark was complemented by OpenHands’ updated software-engineering benchmark announcement (tweet [ https://substack.com/redirect/a461d685-8b2d-45a1-8e53-f017279ae3fb?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]) and Claw-Eval’s more agentic task mix across office, finance, terminal, and web tasks, where MiMo-V2.5-Pro led and DeepSeek V4 Flash looked unusually efficient for its size [ https://substack.com/redirect/3886d136-569e-4026-bf72-291543fc5d62?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ].
TurboQuant skepticism is increasing: Multiple posts pointed to a more sober view of the recently popular quantization/serving technique. @_EldarKurtic [ https://substack.com/redirect/6d659d5d-5303-44ab-8da7-0cc1afab8d92?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] presented what he described as the first comprehensive study of TurboQuant, covering accuracy, latency, and throughput; @vllm_project [ https://substack.com/redirect/717ea216-eced-412a-b102-8987acb34075?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] linked the Red Hat / vLLM investigation as a starting point; and @jbhuang0604 [ https://substack.com/redirect/ff42610d-8091-4280-8b07-9555dc7dafd9?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] bluntly summarized the takeaway as “it doesn’t really work well.” This is exactly the sort of infra claim where independent reproduction matters.
Local/open models continue to improve faster than hardware ceilings: @ClementDelangue [ https://substack.com/redirect/85f9e43a-1ab2-4527-ad77-db7f76aa4dd8?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] made the strongest high-level argument here: on the same top-end MacBook Pro memory ceiling, the “smartest open-weight model you can actually run” improved from Llama 3 70B-era capability to DeepSeek V4 Flash mixed-Q2 GGUF-era capability at roughly 4.7x in 24 months, implying a doubling every 10.7 months, faster than Moore’s Law. Supporting datapoints came from @victormustar [ https://substack.com/redirect/664844cf-b25d-43c4-af61-33d7ac93ea70?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] on the rapid growth of GGUF uploads and from repeated community observations that Qwen 3.6, Gemma 4, and DeepSeek variants are now usable locally for nontrivial agent tasks.
Research Highlights: MoE Modularity, Diffusion/Byte Models, and Agent Dynamics
Architectures and evaluation: AllenAI’s EMO was highlighted by @TheTuringPost [ https://substack.com/redirect/bf08bf2f-8ee4-4b67-8f36-0b4bcff8f71e?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] as a more modular Mixture-of-Experts design where document-level routing induces shared expert pools; notably, keeping only 25% of experts reportedly costs just ~1% performance versus 10–15% degradation in standard MoEs under similar pruning (follow-up [ https://substack.com/redirect/fc8a138e-8bcd-4904-afde-57059807aeb9?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ]). On generative evaluation, @qberthet [ https://substack.com/redirect/9e0ad299-dadd-49bf-b2ec-e9c1161c8309?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] introduced MIND (Monge Inception Distance) as a purportedly faster, more sample-efficient replacement for FID.
Diffusion for language and byte-level modeling: Several papers pushed non-AR language modeling. @LucaAmb [ https://substack.com/redirect/d2ac465d-9172-479f-b449-394632904aa8?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] reported continuous bitstream diffusion nearly matching autoregressive models under their evaluation setup; @JulieKallini [ https://substack.com/redirect/e72c3b63-7942-4dbc-8bfe-0d4bd3e93c89?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] introduced Fast BLT, using diffusion for parallel byte decoding to make byte-level LMs less inference-bound; @sriniiyer88 [ https://substack.com/redirect/a16ba0ed-544f-4833-a330-570aae7a1866?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] framed it as combining block byte-diffusion with self-speculative decoding. Relatedly, @LiangZheng_06 [ https://substack.com/redirect/12d7a203-80f6-4c49-8846-6614a63a0d21?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] noted a useful property of diffusion models for post-training: because sampling is differentiable, reward gradients can in principle flow straight to parameters more directly than in standard LLM setups.
Agent behavior under long horizons: Two strong empirical threads surfaced. First, “The Memory Curse” [ https://substack.com/redirect/af1f588e-0513-4f59-991e-04318a32bda4?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] claims long histories degrade cooperation in multi-round social dilemmas because models become more history-following and risk-minimizing, with explicit CoT sometimes amplifying the problem. Second, PwC work summarized by @dair_ai [ https://substack.com/redirect/543ae1cd-66c9-4e62-9654-21ac5f8df75a?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] argues that the value of clarification is highly time-dependent: goal clarification loses most of its value after ~10% of execution, while input clarification remains useful longer. Together these suggest that long-horizon agent quality is constrained as much by memory/control policy as by raw model IQ.
Scaling and self-improvement: Marin’s Delphi scaling work, summarized by @WilliamBarrHeld [ https://substack.com/redirect/da0b2766-9673-4c63-a11f-9a53ca736a6c?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ], claims a 0.2% prediction error when extrapolating from small pretrains to a 25B / 600B token run. Separately, @omarsar0 [ https://substack.com/redirect/49947636-c6f5-4b66-95e3-7f89a19556a8?j=eyJ1IjoiODlhdDlmIn0.psR-epQqK_Wbg6RTI-yQkSgRmDIqosMhU9elL-EoqO0 ] highlighted AutoTTS, where an LLM searches the test-time scaling controller space itself, reportedly beating hand-designed strategies for about $39.9 of discovery cost.
