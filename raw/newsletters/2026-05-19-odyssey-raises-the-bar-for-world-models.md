**1. Odyssey drops two world models in one day:** The AI lab’s release pushes world models into audio and multiplayer territory. [Agora-1](https://odyssey.ml/introducing-agora-1) lets multiple human or AI participants interact inside the same simulation in real time — try it via a [playable GoldenEye-style game.](https://agora.odyssey.ml/) The second model, [Starchild-1](https://odyssey.ml/introducing-starchild-1), generates audio alongside its visuals, and is being billed as the first real-time multimodal world model. [See how it works here.](https://x.com/odysseyml/status/2056416808580816957)

**2. Apple bets on auto-deleting chats to differentiate Siri:** Apple's revamped standalone Siri app (debuting in iOS 27) will [reportedly](https://www.engadget.com/2174928/apple-new-siri-app-will-reportedly-offer-auto-deleting-chat-options/?utm_campaign=apple-siri-auto-deleting-chats&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=superhuman) let users auto-delete chat histories after a set time period. This privacy-first approach would set Apple apart from its rivals, which typically retain conversations unless you specifically opt out. Expect the new Siri to drop at [WWDC 2026](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/) on June 8.

**3. Cursor ships Composer 2.5 and teases a SpaceXAI collab:** Cursor just dropped [Composer 2.5](https://cursor.com/blog/composer-2-5), its most powerful in-house coding model yet, matching frontier models on key benchmarks at a fraction of the price. It’s live now with doubled usage for the first week. The AI leader also announced that it’s working on a “significantly larger model” with SpaceXAI using 10x more compute.

## **Gen Z’s AI sentiment has soured since last year —  and commencement speakers have learned the hard way**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/d3f2ab48-e6b6-47f7-a5f0-b2bd7b187ddc/superhumanteam_Graduates_booing_their_commencement_speaker_--_97c2fa6d-906d-4059-b5a0-63625770f49a_3.jpg?t=1779121099)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**Decreasing optimism. **Gen Z's excitement over AI has [slipped](https://news.gallup.com/poll/708224/gen-adoption-steady-skepticism-climbs.aspx) from 36% to 22% year-over-year, fueled by AI’s impact on jobs, the environment, and creativity. And that shift isn't just showing up in polls — it's playing out in tense moments at college graduations across the country.

**Commencement speeches. **Tavistock Group VP Gloria Caulfield called AI "the next industrial revolution" during a speech at the University of Central Florida. The students’ response? [Immediate boos](https://x.com/ABC/status/2054394594733748593). Ex-Google CEO Eric Schmidt got a [similar reaction](https://x.com/Kantrowitz/status/2056042363659677791) every time he mentioned AI at the University of Arizona, although students had also protested his appearance beforehand over non-AI-related issues.

**Why the shift?** Schmidt actually acknowledged the elephant in the room during his speech: "There is a fear that the machines are coming, the jobs are evaporating, the climate is breaking, politics are fractured, and that you are inheriting a mess that you did not create." Many graduates blame AI, at least partly, for those fears.

**Threading the needle. **Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a different experience, with no boos or pushback during his speech at Carnegie Mellon. Huang’s framing? AI isn’t coming for students’ jobs. It’s unlocking unprecedented opportunity by letting them build any technology they want.
