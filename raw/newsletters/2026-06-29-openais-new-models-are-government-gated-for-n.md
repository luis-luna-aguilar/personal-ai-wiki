**1. OpenAI launches GPT-5.6 series to a limited audience: **The [GPT-5.6 model series](https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/) includes Sol, OpenAI’s strongest model yet, Terra, Sol’s more affordable daily driver, and Luna, a budget option. At the U.S. government's request, OpenAI is opening to a limited circle of partners first to ensure safety. This mirrors the treatment of Anthropic’s Mythos, which is [now back](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/26/tech/anthropic-mythos-release) in limited preview, with Fable [rumored](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/27/anthropic-fable-5-return-soon) to be released this week as well.

**2. New Chinese models close the gap with America’s frontier labs: **Tulongfeng is a new model from Chinese cybersecurity company 360 Security Technology that its founder [billed](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/china-claims-developed-ai-cyber-112918942.html) as a "Chinese version of Mythos” when it comes to identifying cyber bugs. The recent release of Z.ai’s [GLM-5.2](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.2) has also put pressure on US frontier labs by rivaling top model performance at a fraction of the price. [Read more.](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/chinese-ai-anthropic-mythos-cybersecurity-574b02c2?st=V93N2F&mod=superhuman)

**3. Google rations Meta's access to Gemini:** Google has [capped](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-caps-meta-gemini-compute-shortage) Meta's use of its Gemini AI models, citing a lack of compute capacity. Google is so compute constrained that it even agreed to pay SpaceX $920 million a month to access 110,000 Nvidia GPUs. The situation highlights how the AI infrastructure buildout has not kept pace with consumption, and that the ongoing compute shortage is starting to reshape corporate relationships.

##### **FROM THE FRONTIER**

## **AI continues to mint winners and gut losers in the same stock market **


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/ba45fea3-7c4c-46bb-9785-fbc7e69c7497/superhumanteam_A_robot_with_a_rising_arrow_on_one_side_and_fa_5650dc7f-09c8-44e6-819c-76fbdf668af7_3.jpg?t=1782664784)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**Memory chips surge. **Semiconductor and memory chip firms haven’t been able to lose this year. Last week, Micron (+296% YTD) reported that quarterly revenue quadrupled to nearly $42B, SanDisk (+780% YTD) saw last quarter's sales jump 251% to $6B, and SK Hynix (+294% YTD) has grown so quickly it’s now South Korea’s most valuable company. But these companies have been the exception in the stock market lately — with many marquee tech names far below their all-time highs.

**Software’s bad year continues.** Earlier this year, fears that AI would disrupt enterprise software (known as the [SaaSpocalypse](https://www.thesaascfo.com/the-saaspocalypse-ai-agents-vibe-coding-and-the-changing-economics-of-saas/)) sent names like Oracle, Salesforce, and ServiceNow cratering. Months later, all three are still down more than 50% from their all-time highs, although the White House’s move to restrict OpenAI's new GPT-5.6 models triggered a sharp increase last Friday.

**Big Tech stumbles.** Meta (-29%), Amazon (-15%), Alphabet (-16%), and Microsoft (-28%) have all slipped from their highs in recent weeks. One catalyst: high spending. These companies plan to sink [roughly](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/big-techs-ai-spending-plans-reach-725-billion) $725B into AI infrastructure this year, with a growing part of the bill being funded by debt. Investors have pulled back until there’s more proof this spending is paying off.

**But the market is green.** For all the red, the S&P 500, Dow Jones, and Nasdaq are still up 7 to 9% on the year — far far from recession territory. Still, expect volatility to be a key theme for the remainder of 2026, as a single model launch, feature, or leaked memo can swing markets hard in either direction.

## **How to turn Claude Cowork into your personalized assistant**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/db0e9347-db5c-4c7c-8c19-d67b44952641/image__9_.jpg?t=1782663341)
Caption: 


--------------------
* Go to [Claude](https://claude.com/download) and sign in with your account.

* Go to Cowork → Dispatch → Enable to control Claude Cowork on your PC directly from your phone.

* Go to Instructions > enter your personalized instructions and click Save.

**_Sample Prompt:_**_ I want to create a custom Al assistant settings document I can paste into my AI tool (like Claude Cowork, a custom GPT system prompt, or similar). First, assess what you already know about me from our conversation history. If you have enough to write something specific and opinionated, go ahead and draft it. If there are gaps that would make the output generic, ask me only the questions you actually need answered - don't interview me for information you already have. Cover: how I like to work, my communication style and tone preferences, what I'm building or managing, how I want the AI to handle ambiguity or pushback, what kinds of decisions or actions require my explicit approval before proceeding, and any recurring workflows or contexts the AI should know upfront. The output should be a formatted settings doc I can paste directly in - specific to me, not boilerplate. Try to condense because this is loaded every time._

* These instructions will automatically apply across all future Claude Cowork sessions.

* Then go ahead and try to perform a task with Cowork to get improved results.
