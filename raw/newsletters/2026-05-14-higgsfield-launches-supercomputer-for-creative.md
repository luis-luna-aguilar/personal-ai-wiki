##### **FROM THE FRONTIER**

## **A Nobel laureate's machine can pull 4,000 liters of water from thin air per day — and it could solve AI's water problem.**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/3b872c64-4159-4983-b0fb-a69ab4c9f6fc/superhumanteam_A_scientist_and_a_gigantic_machine_that_sucks__ec853024-f80b-4026-ba9b-597f5f3ab372_3.jpg?t=1778696307)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**Thirsty servers. **Data centers are on track to [guzzle](https://www.issa.com/industry-news/ai-data-center-water-consumption-is-creating-an-unprecedented-crisis-in-the-united-states/) 68 billion gallons of water annually by 2028. That’s a 300% increase in five years, and an issue that’s starting to cause problems across the US. But one startup might have an answer: harvesting water directly from the atmosphere.

**Atoco’s solution.** Atoco’s machine uses metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) — microscopic structures that trap specific molecules at the atomic level — to [pluck](https://atoco.com/atmospheric-water-harvesting/) H2O straight from the air, even in arid regions. Each commercial unit is the size of a shipping container and produces about 4,000 liters of water daily. It can even run on waste heat from machinery — turning a cooling problem into a water source.

**Meet the chemist.** Atoco was founded by [Omar Yaghi](https://atoco.com/our-founder/), a UC Berkeley professor who shared the 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on MOFs. Yaghi boasts 55 prestigious awards and has published 300+ peer-reviewed articles. He now oversees Atoco’s R&D department.

**Launching soon.** Atoco says the atmosphere holds far more water than any harvester could pull, so its system won’t disrupt the water cycle. Production starts later this year, and potential customers aren’t limited to AI companies — any region facing water shortages could benefit.
