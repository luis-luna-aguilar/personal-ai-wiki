High effort spins up sub-agents to verify every single finding. Anthropic says even the lowest tier outperforms rival tools. Update Claude Code and try it on your next PR.

## **Claude Code creator explains how to build an AI-native engineering team**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/260c1834-41fc-42b3-9010-e193c2b3d739/superhumanteam_a_software_engineering_team_working_on_one_eng_70565ac5-787f-476d-8852-9f734d244895_3.jpg?t=1784278570)

**The lone sprinter problem.** AI adoption follows a familiar pattern at most companies. One engineer might be 10x'ing their output with Claude Code. Meanwhile, the rest of the org hasn't caught up yet. Claude Code creator Boris Cherny hears [**this story**](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2077929379661844559) every single day. So he mapped out the exact roadmap teams take to close that gap.

**Four steps to AI native adoption. **His [map](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/bfdfaef9-bc62-4dfe-ba9e-c58a26c9accf) tracks how the engineer's role changes:

* Step one pairs an engineer with a single agent. The engineer reviews every change before it merges.

* Step two turns that engineer into an orchestrator. They juggle ten agents at once.

* Step three is supervised autonomy. The engineer acts as a manager of managers with a hundred agents running.

* Step four is AI native. Thousands of agents run at once, and Claude kicks off most of them itself.

**Bottlenecks not budgets.** The real question is what actually scales a team from one stage to the next. Simply buying more tokens won't cut it. The biggest challenge is domain knowledge that lives in your engineer’s head. To overcome this, Cherny recommends [documenting ](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2077460395279692197)everything and building markdown files for your agents. It ensures that technical knowledge doesn't just sit in silos but becomes an active asset for the entire team.

**Every line earns its place.** Most teams struggle when it comes to documenting domain expertise.The natural instinct is to over-document, but models start losing the plot after a few hundred lines. HumanLayer's [guide](https://www.humanlayer.dev/blog/writing-a-good-claude-md) shows how to keep a CLAUDE.md inside that budget, and Anthropic’s guide on [writing effective skills](https://resources.anthropic.com/hubfs/The-Complete-Guide-to-Building-Skill-for-Claude.pdf) will help you document deeper workflows that your agents can use when needed.

## **How to stop test coverage rotting in Cursor**

Code merges faster than tests can be written. Once a PR is merged, it's rarely touched again. The Cursor engineering team solves this with a scheduled agent. Their **[blog explains](https://cursor.com/blog/automations)** how they use morning automation to review recent merges and fill in missing tests.

You can set this up with a single prompt in Cursor 3.8 or later:

```
/automate

Every morning, review code merged in the last 24 hours
and identify areas that need test coverage. Add tests
following existing conventions. Do not alter production
behavior. Open a PR with the new tests.
```
Cursor builds the schedule and tools from that description. Every morning, you can review a small test-only PR.
