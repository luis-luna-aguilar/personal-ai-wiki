## **The job market is frozen — but Ramp’s data shows AI-forward employers may be the exception**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/df25f442-8ccb-4a06-9f24-4ce44e8bd486/superhumanteam_A_robot_stuck_in_an_ice_cube_--ar_21_--sref_ht_2be850ca-484c-4d96-bd84-8c9c96caea3b_0.jpg?t=1782865596)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**Two labor markets.** US job openings held flat at 7.6M last May, unchanged from the previous month, according to the [Bureau of Labor Statistics.](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm) At the macro level, the labor market looks frozen. But underneath the freeze, a study from Ramp paints a slightly more optimistic picture of the labor market.

**Emerging growth.** Ramp [analyzed](https://ramp.com/data/ai-jobs-impact) roughly 22,000 companies and discovered that firms spending heavily on AI (considered to be $30 per employee per month) grew headcount at 10.2% in the two years following their adoption of AI. Entry-level roles grew even faster at 12%, while firms with low adoption saw no growth. 

**The catch.** The paper's authors admit this data isn’t perfect. It skews heavily towards tech-forward, venture-backed firms that were likely already growing and hiring. But the authors were adamant on one point: while AI may not be creating jobs just yet, the data shows that it’s not destroying them either.

**Reshaping work.** Amazon Web Services CEO ==Matt Garman has a ====[theory](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/amazon-exec-says-ai-won-151430813.html)====: AI isn’t eliminating roles, just reshaping them. ==Garman added,== =="I do think that half of white-collar jobs may change, but wipe out and change are different.” His comparison? Microsoft Excel — it eliminated manual-calculation jobs, but sparked a need for higher-level analyst roles.
