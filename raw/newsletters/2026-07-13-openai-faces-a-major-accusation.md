## **There’s a hidden cost of using AI — and corporate America is getting tired of paying it**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/a1d74d66-f3b6-45fd-b1e7-afcc2e701576/superhumanteam_A_business_man_with_his_arms_folded_looking_at_87f16099-7227-415c-94e0-115bfbf39e76_2.jpg?t=1783903735)
Caption: Made with Midjourney


--------------------
**AI’s hidden cost.** Using AI has always carried a financial price tag — either a monthly subscription or an API token bill. But as enterprises continue to weave AI into their workflows, they're also paying with something far more valuable than money: proprietary corporate knowledge, handed over in tiny increments every time they run a prompt.

**The case for AI sovereignty. **Two weeks ago, Palantir argued that “AI sovereignty” (or an institution's control over its own data and technical decisions) is becoming existential. The [nine-point manifesto](https://x.com/PalantirTech/status/2072114267776491695) warned companies that handing proprietary data to AI providers means surrendering their competitive edge, control, and, potentially, their future. Microsoft's Satya Nadella just concurred.

**Protecting corporate IP. **Satya Nadella just gave the dilemma a name: [the Reverse Information Paradox](https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2076323181154230284). He argues that today's AI ecosystem creates a one-sided flow of information, where model providers learn how their customers operate with every interaction. Over time, that accumulated learning becomes a significant advantage.

**What’s the solution?** Both leaders point in the same direction: enterprises need the ability to fine-tune and train their own models while keeping their data, weights, and memory in-house.
