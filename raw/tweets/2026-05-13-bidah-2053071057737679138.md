# ROFI on X: "Drop your screenshots and this Claude skill turns them into animated onboarding"

Let me give you the single biggest lever I've seen for mobile app conversions: you need videos showing your app's functionality during onboarding — before the paywall. That's it. But most importantly, Not after. Not on the paywall screen as a background animation or anything like that. BEFORE.

Two reasons, both backed by user behavior most indie app developers underestimate:

Mobile users have learned to assume the worst. They've been burned by apps that ship a glossy App Store listing and then deliver an empty shell behind a $9.99/month paywall. By the time someone reaches your paywall screen, they've already decided whether they trust you. Static screenshots and bullet points don't move that needle. Watching the product \*actually work\* does.

2. It clarifies what your app does

This sounds obvious, but most apps fail it. Developers (I am guilty here) are too close to their product or just thinking about code, design, etc to notice that a new user opening the app for the first time has no idea what the core loop is of your app and if it really is going to solve their PAIN. That is because just the first moment a user really \*understands\* what your app is for is when they make the thought of buying your solution a thing. If that moment doesn't happen until after they pay, you've already lost most of them. Just a few will remain. Other don't know you, don't trust you (why would they?). They will close your app... forever.

What does “show value” actually mean?
For me it’s simple: show the core features through video during onboarding. Clear, visual, no guessing.

Take "video onboarding" seriously and jump into the world of screen recording. It's easy, just open the simulator, hit record, tap through the feature, and add it on a screen on onboarding with H1, and a sub H2, explaining the feature.

Ok now, so this works, technically, but it misses the point. A raw screen recording shows \*that\* the feature works. It doesn't teach.

Viktor's move is to treat onboarding videos as didactic content, not documentation. Instead of just recording app usage, he creates small videos that \*replicate\* the app experience in a more granular, more deliberate way. Pointer animations exaggerated for clarity. Pauses on the screens that matter. Tap targets highlighted before the action happens. The user is being taught what the app does, not shown a clip of someone else using it.

That distinction — show vs. teach — is something Viktor does not mention so clearly on his post but it is implicit. There is thin line that separates onboarding that converts from onboarding that gets skipped by creating a demo video format that matches this idea.

The catch is that producing this kind of video is real work. You need design taste, motion skills, the right tools, and the patience to redo a clip seven times when the timing is off. Most teams don't have that bandwidth — especially solo founders and small mobile teams shipping their tenth feature this quarter. So they default to raw screen recordings, or skip onboarding videos entirely, and eat the conversion hit.

So I'm releasing a skill to close that gap:

The flow is intentionally simple: drop in a sequence of screenshots of your feature, in order. The skill does the rest.

• Pointer animations for finger movements and tap actions, generated to match the flow you're demonstrating

• A regenerated, interactive UI based on your designs — not a flat screen capture, but a reconstructed version that can pause, highlight, and emphasize the steps that matter

• A polished walkthrough video ready to drop into your onboarding flow, before the paywall
