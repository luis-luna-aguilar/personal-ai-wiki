# Proposal: GPT-6.1 Terra — OpenAI's current fast, low-cost tier

## Summary

### In short
OpenAI's line-up has a current tier the wiki does not describe: GPT-6.1 Terra, launched 26 October at $1/$5 per million tokens and pitched for computer use and everyday writing, sits between GPT-6.2 Sol and GPT-6 Luna. The November GPT-6.2 release refreshed only Astra and Sol, so Terra is still current. Every, the first outside reader, skipped a full review because its writing gain looked small. The proposal creates a `models/gpt-6-1-terra.md` page, adds it to Small / fast models on **State of Models** and creates two source pages. The reason to hesitate: State of Models goes just past its length ceiling.

### The source
On 26 October 2026 OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, which BleepingComputer covered the same day from OpenAI's blog post. OpenAI calls it "a fast, efficient model" that combines detailed visual reasoning and precise computer use with clearer, more natural writing, aimed at the work that fills a day: drafting proposals, reading technical drawings, filling out forms and editing videos. It costs $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, half the price of GPT-5.6 Terra and of the mid tier at the time. It is available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, and in the API as `gpt-6.1-terra`.

OpenAI's numbers compare it mostly with its predecessor. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, a command-line task benchmark, it scores 50.3% at maximum reasoning effort, against 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra. On Agents' Last Exam it gains 15 points over GPT-5.6 Terra at medium effort at about a third of the cost. On an internal factual-error test its error rate at low effort falls from 53.6% to 16.7%, and to 4.3% at maximum effort. OpenAI says it finishes tasks more than three times faster than its larger models of the time, and still recommends those larger models for the hardest coding.

Three days later Every's newsletter advised readers to skip it. Dan Shipper's team "didn't see enough of a difference" to run a full Vibe Check. Writer Grant Harvey found Terra's first newsletter draft full of AI tells, but it took notes well over the second and third drafts, which makes it "a capable iterative writer that still needs a lot of human steering". The same item says Terra is fast at simple computer tasks such as installing Chrome but fails at complex ones such as setting up that very article in Every's content system, then adds that no one at Every had tested its computer use yet.

### What changes
Terra appears in the wiki only as a name in the discounted background-processing list; there is no model page and no State of Models entry.

### What to weigh
OpenAI's launch post itself was not fetched; its figures come through BleepingComputer's report. OpenAI's comparisons with GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol are left out of the page because both models have been replaced; only Terra's own scores stay. Every's item contradicts itself on computer use, describing a failed CMS task while saying nobody had tested it; the page reports both statements as written. The added line takes State of Models from 1,992 to about 2,030 body words, a warning-level 101% of its 2,000 ceiling.

# GPT-6.1 Terra

OpenAI's fast, low-cost tier, launched 2026-10-26 and pitched for computer use, visual reasoning and everyday writing: forms, technical drawings, video edits and proposal drafts. It sits between [GPT-6.2 Sol](gpt-6-2-sol.md) and [GPT-6 Luna](gpt-6-luna.md). The GPT-6.2 release refreshed only Astra and Sol, so Terra remains current.

## Current status (as of 2026-10-29)

- **Price:** $1/$5 per 1M input/output tokens, half GPT-5.6 Terra's price. Discounted background windows apply ([OpenAI discounted async processing](../workflows/async-discounted-processing.md)).
- **Availability:** Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu in ChatGPT Work and Codex; API as `gpt-6.1-terra`.
- **OpenAI's launch numbers:** [Terminal-Bench](../benchmarks/terminal-bench.md) 4.0 50.3% at max effort (GPT-5.6 Terra 21.5%); [Agents' Last Exam](../benchmarks/agents-last-exam.md) +15 points over GPT-5.6 Terra at medium effort for about a third of the cost; internal factual-error rate 16.7% at low effort (GPT-5.6 Terra 53.6%) and 4.3% at max. OpenAI says it is more than three times faster than its larger models and still recommends those for the hardest coding.
- **Every's read (2026-10-29): skip.** Too little writing gain to justify a Vibe Check; first drafts full of AI tells, but it revised well from notes, so a capable iterative writer that needs steering.

## Weaknesses / caveats

- OpenAI's comparisons at launch were against [GPT-6 Astra](../history/models/gpt-6-astra.md) and [GPT-6.1 Sol](../history/models/gpt-6-1-sol.md), both since replaced; no comparison with the 6.2 models is captured.
- Computer use is the pitch, but the only outside report is unclear: Every's newsletter says it is fast at simple tasks (installing Chrome) and fails complex ones (setting up that article in Every's CMS), then says no one at Every had tested its computer use yet.
