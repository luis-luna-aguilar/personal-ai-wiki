- [x] **[models]** Decision models become a product category: TypeSafe's Jev launches "System One" models, then Cloudflare Clef, OpenAI's Decisions API, open rivals and an Artificial Analysis Decision Index follow within a month. **High priority (20 sources across three digests).**

    **What it is:** On 2026-09-15 TypeSafe (CEO Diogo Almeida, a co-author of InstructGPT) released Jev, a model that classifies instead of generating text. It returns a probability for a yes/no question (a "Noulli"), a multiple-choice pick ("Choice") or a rating ("Score"). Answers come back in parallel and almost at once, at $0.042 per million input tokens with output free. Almeida's long Latent Space interview covers the training objective, RLCD ("reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions", meaning the model is trained to give honest probabilities rather than answers human raters like) and his argument that RLHF causes mode collapse and miscalibration. It also covers his refusal to publish public benchmarks, a no-refusals stance for an API, his advice to break AI workflows into many small checkable decisions, and his "tyranny of the KV cache" critique of single-model coding agents. Every's how-to adds practical rules: send a task to Jev if a person could decide it in under 10 seconds, and turn a vague question such as "is this urgent?" into several weighted yes/no checks that you test against items you have already judged. In the ecosystem, a Jev-as-a-judge paper reports $0.044 per 1K judgments at 152 ms, about 277x cheaper than GPT-6, and within 3 points on RewardBench and HaluEval but 14.5 points behind on JudgeBench. Ramp matched GPT-5.6 Luna reranking at a third of the cost, LangSmith added decision-model tracing, and TypeSafe is reportedly raising $1B+ at a $10B+ valuation. OpenAI's Decisions API, a Luna wrapper built in about a week, scored 76/78 steps against Jev's 73 in Every's replay test. CLM-8B is a contrastive state-action model released with open weights. It claims 9x faster inference than Jev and SOTA verifier scores after fine-tuning (DeepSWE 81.6%, Terminal-Bench 2.1 87.6%), but has weaker zero-shot breadth and shorter calibrated context. A Reddit post shows a BGE-small plus logistic-regression baseline beating Jev on Banking77 (93.3% vs 83.2%).

    In early October the category filled out. Cloudflare released Clef and Clef-flash (Apache 2.0): a frozen Qwen does one prefill pass and a small schema head scores every option. Clef is Jev-API compatible, comes with an RL fine-tuning product and a later clef-omni for audio/video, and Cloudflare says it leads the "Jev Decision Index" (a Mia tweet claims "4x faster than Jev at 2x the accuracy on some benchmarks"). OpenAI's Decisions API went to beta on GPT-6 Luna at $0.10/M input with no output charge, "up to 10x faster" than Responses; Dan Shipper called it potentially "the biggest release of the day if it's cheap". Perplexity open-sourced pplx-decider-v1.1-27b ($0.02/M input; tops the HF Decision Index v0.3; 643/669 clinical decisions vs 628 for Jev), followed in mid-October by pplx-decider v1.2 (27B and 4B). Others: Command Code Agr (31B / 360M), Microsoft-Decision-1, Liquid d1 on Vercel, Mercury Decide, Bespoke's Nimble-v4, vLLM Semantic Router Decision 2.0 and then a `/v1/systemone` endpoint in vLLM v0.32, OpenAI's Live API adding Decisions, and an Unsloth notebook that turns Qwen3.5-4B into a decision model on 8GB. ScaleDown rebranded its family as "Constrained Decoding Models" (77.2% DecisionBench vs Jev's 72%; claims 200x faster and 467x cheaper than frontier APIs). On 2026-10-14 Artificial Analysis launched its own Decision Index (knowledge, reasoning, tool calling, safety classification): Jev 1.13 led at 58 until Liquid d1-ultra took the top at 65 (92ms/task, $0.042/M input, free output), with Haiku 5.5 at 61 for about 4x the cost and 10x the latency. AA finds these models competitive on classification and routing but weaker and overconfident on knowledge and reasoning. Other independent checks: Vals found Jev matched GPT-6 Astra's 97.5% on claim verification at about 1/500th the cost but ranked last on LegalBench; LangChain says routing each step to the cheapest adequate model cut median Open SWE cost per task by 64%; Theo calls routing-by-intelligence uses "useless".

    **Why it matters:** The wiki has no entity page for Jev and no page for the category. Jev appears only as a comparator on `models/scaledown.md`, `models/meddecider-27b.md` and `tools/expand-fetch.md`, and the "Domain-specialized models" section of `state-of/models.md` holds only individual members. This is a new model class with its own benchmark now (three different indices: Cloudflare's Jev Decision Index claim, HF Decision Index v0.3, and AA's Decision Index), so a category page such as `concepts/decision-models.md` plus a Jev page is likely, linking into `training/cost-aware-ai-task-routing.md` (cheap judges, routing, cascades). The proposal will need a subcategory decision, since none of the current ones fit a non-generative decision model.

- [x] **[models]** Claude Opus 5.5: launch date, third-party evals, and the hands-on verdict. **Update only.**

    **What it is:** Opus 5.5 launched on 2026-09-22, the same day as GPT-6 Sol. AINews switched its own pipeline to it. Third-party results: #1 on the Vals Index and Vals RSI Index; CursorBench 57.8% (new top); FrontierCode 1.1 Extended 65.3% (#1); FrontierSWE 62.3% (#2 behind Astra); #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at 66 (Terminal-Bench 4.0 63.1%); SimpleBench 88.4%; and a record writing-benchmark Elo. The price cut to $4/$20 is offset by higher token use. Artificial Analysis's breakdown shows the per-task saving over Opus 5 disappears at max effort, so the "40% cheaper" claim holds only at the default medium effort. On FrontierCode, xhigh effort costs 2.8x more than medium for a 3.2-point lower score, attributed to scope creep. ProgramBench's author flagged that Anthropic's near-100% figure comes from a 166/200 subset and uses a pass-rate metric. Opus 5.5 is the first Opus with Fable-class safeguards: flagged requests fall back to another model, and there were early over-trigger complaints.
