- [x] **[training]** Optimized writing skills beat descriptive style guides — Mike Taylor's tested method (briefs, pairwise judge, GEPA)

    **What it is:** Mike Taylor tested four ways to build a "write like me" skill on 185 of his posts (54 held out). A Claude-written style guide lost to all three optimizers (Karpathy's autoresearch, DSPy's GEPA, Every's ce-optimize) on 80–95% of held-out posts, and for Opus 5.5 no skill beat the descriptive skill 61% of the time (the guide produced a caricature of his "greatest hits"). Method: turn each post into a style-free brief; judge with a "pairwise gold triad" (real post plus two drafts, order swapped to cancel position bias); keep only skill edits that score better. Judge consistency on swapped order: Opus 5.5 86%, Sonnet 5.5 70%, Jev 50%. Optimizing cost $27–53 per skill against $5 one-shot, with most gains in three rounds; GEPA won (77–31 vs autoresearch, 85–15 vs ce-optimize). Skills are model-specific: Opus 5.5 went from 16% with no skill to 93% with its GEPA skill, and Sonnet 5.5 with an optimized skill beat Opus 5.5 with the generic one on 46 of 60 judgments. A companion "Writing Arena" result: a one-pass skill lost to just handing the model a folder of the writer's posts for all five writers tested, until 10+ optimization rounds flipped four of them. Caveat from his personal-benchmark talk: optimized checks can overfit (a "cat sits facing front" rule that broke a curled-up-cat request).

    **Why it matters:** The wiki's AI style-guide page says build guides from real samples and keep them short; it has no evidence that descriptive guides underperform optimized ones, no brief-plus-pairwise-judge recipe, and no judge-consistency numbers. Updates `training/ai-style-guides.md`, with a judge note for `training/evals-for-agentic-work.md` and an optimizer example for `concepts/automated-harness-optimization.md` (self-improving skills).
