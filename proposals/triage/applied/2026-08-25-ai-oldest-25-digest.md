- [x] **[training]** Dan Shipper's "After Automation": AI creates more work for humans, not less

    **What it is:** Every CEO Dan Shipper's essay argues AI progress increases demand for human expert work rather than eliminating it. Core mechanism: models are trained on the "residue" of past human competence, which becomes cheap and widely available; cheap competence floods in and produces "sameness" (slop); sameness creates demand for differentiated, human-judgment-driven work; and this repeats even under strong AGI because there is always a human "framer" behind any goal a model optimizes toward (the "frame is not the framer" argument). Includes concrete internal-Every examples: a "Senior Engineer benchmark" showing GPT-5.5 scoring 62/100 (30 points above Opus 4.7, still ~30 points below human senior engineers); the "human sandwich" pattern (human frames the task, agent collapses it, human judges/extends the result); agent employees (coworker agents like Claudie/Andy/Viktor vs. embedded agents like Fin, which closed 40.1% of actionable support conversations without a human in a recent week); and a critique of "chart psychosis" — benchmarks measure performance inside a chosen frame, and saturating one frame just shifts demand to the next frame, not to zero human work.
    **Why it matters:** Strong, well-reasoned framework directly relevant to `training/ai-work-delegation-modes.md`, `training/ai-delegation-management.md`, and `concepts/agent-evals.md` (the benchmark-framing critique).
