# Open-weight economics fragment by deployment constraint

AINews groups Granite, Ant OSS Ling, Hunyuan, and various pricing signals into a broader structural pattern: open-weight model economics are not converging — they are fragmenting by deployment constraint. Different open models optimize for different tradeoffs, and the right choice increasingly depends on where and how you deploy.

## Key claims extracted

- IBM Granite, Ant OSS Ling, and Hunyuan are cited as examples of open-weight models with differentiated positioning
- Fragmentation axes identified: transparency (model card depth, training data disclosure), token efficiency (output/input token ratio), edge deployment (hardware requirements), coding benchmarks, and inference cost
- The pattern: no single open-weight model dominates across all axes; operators must choose based on their specific deployment constraint
- Pricing signals show divergence: some open models have low output-token costs while others are surprisingly expensive at scale

## Caveats

- AINews synthesis; model-specific claims require verification against IBM, Alibaba, and Tencent documentation
- Fragmentation framing is analytical; not backed by a systematic cross-model benchmark study
